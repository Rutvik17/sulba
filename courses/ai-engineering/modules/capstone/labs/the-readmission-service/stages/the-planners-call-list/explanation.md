The assistant's tests used a day of stays that each had an HbA1c result, none of them a death or hospice care, and fewer than a batch, so they passed. A real day has all three: most stays have no HbA1c test, a busy day runs to more than one batch, and some patients die or go into hospice care. Each problem shows up only on such a day, and two of them would have stopped the list altogether.

---

None of these would have shown in the service's own tests, since each lies in how a client reads its file and splits its stays. The service did its part, refusing what it can't answer for. The client turned those refusals into a list with nothing on it, or with stays missing that no one would know to look for.
