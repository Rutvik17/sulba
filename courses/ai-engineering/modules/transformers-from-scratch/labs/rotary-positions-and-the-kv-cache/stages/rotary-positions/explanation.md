A query turned through `$m\theta_i$` and a key turned through `$n\theta_i$` meet in each pair at the angle between them, `$(m - n)\theta_i$`, so their dot product depends on the two vectors and on how far apart the tokens are, not on where the story started. That is what the third test checks. The angles come from the position and the base alone, where GPT-2 small's learnt positions held {% $fig.gpt2_positions %} numbers.

The {% $fig.pairs %} pairs turn at different speeds. The fastest, pair 0, turns 1 radian a position, once round every {% $fig.fastest_period %} positions, so it tells neighbours apart. The slowest turns once round in {% $fig.slowest_period %} positions, far more than SmolLM2's {% $fig.context %}, so across a whole story it keeps turning one way, and a token near the start stays apart from one near the end.

The paper that introduced rotary positions paired neighbouring numbers, `$2i$` with `$2i + 1$`. The Llama code in Hugging Face's transformers pairs each number with the one half a head away. Both turn the same angles, but a model's weights work with one pairing only, and SmolLM2's `config.json` says which: its `rope_interleaved` is `false`.
