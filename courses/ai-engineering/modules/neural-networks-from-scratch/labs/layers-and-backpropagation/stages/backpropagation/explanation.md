`backward` runs the layers in reverse because each layer's backward pass needs the gradient at its own outputs, and the layer after it is what works that out. Each backward pass reuses what its forward pass kept, so the whole gradient costs about two forward passes more, however many layers there are. This is backpropagation: the chain rule, worked from the loss back to the inputs one layer at a time. PyTorch's autograd does the same: it records each step as the forward pass runs, then works back through them. For the tests' networks, with hidden layers of {% $fig.hidden %} units and a batch of {% $fig.samples %} digits, your gradients agree with PyTorch's.
