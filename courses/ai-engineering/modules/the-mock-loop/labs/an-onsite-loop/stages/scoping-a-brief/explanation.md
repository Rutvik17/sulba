## The problem

Officers spend about {% $fig.officer_hours %} hours a week reading about {% $fig.comments %} comments a year and summarising them for the committee. The aim is to cut that time without the committee missing a point it should hear. That's the measure of success, and it's the council's, not the tool's.

## The first version

For one kind of application, such as householder extensions, the tool groups each application's comments by theme, such as parking, overlooking or noise, with the comments that raise each, and drafts the committee summary in the council's template. An officer reads the draft beside the comments, edits it, and signs it off. Nothing reaches the committee without an officer.

## Left out

Deciding which comments are valid planning objections is a judgment the officers make under planning law, and it stays with them. The tool shows themes and quotes, not verdicts. Replying to residents, and other kinds of application, come later, if the first version works.

## What's needed from the council

- past applications of that kind with their comments and the officers' summaries, to build and test on;
- the committee's template;
- sign-off from the council's data protection officer on handling residents' comments;
- an officer for a few hours each week to review drafts.

## The estimate

Each task gets three numbers, as in Estimating the work: the data, the grouping, the drafts, the review screen, the tests and the pilot. A simulation of the tasks gives a finish at a stated chance, and the scope says whether {% $fig.weeks %} weeks covers it at that chance, and what would be cut if not.

## Measuring

Before the pilot, officers time how long a summary takes now. During it, the same measure on the same kind of application, and the share of the points officers added to each draft. The council sets the hours saved, as in Measuring value, against the cost of the work and of running it.

## Risks

- Residents' comments hold personal data: the tool runs where the council's data protection officer agrees, and keeps nothing it doesn't need.
- The council's rules on decisions made by software: the tool drafts, and officers decide.
- Officers may not trust the drafts: they review every one, and the pilot measures how much they change.

## The follow-up questions

- **Deciding valid objections:** no, not in this scope. It's a legal judgment that's the officers', and a tool that decided it would need far more evidence and sign-off than a summary does. The tool can show the themes officers judge.
- **The committee's template, halfway:** it's a change of scope, so its tasks get their three numbers and the simulation runs again. The council sees the new finish at the same chance and chooses between the date and something to cut.
- **Worth it after {% $fig.review_months %} months:** the officer hours per application before and after, the points officers added to drafts, and the cost of the work and of running it.
