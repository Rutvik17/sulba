A request's address, body and headers are the caller's to write. Only the session, which the college's provider vouched for, says who the caller is, and only the office's directory says which student that is. A way for staff to look students up needs its own sign-in and its own check of who may, not a field any caller can fill.

A stream's errors reach the student as a broken reply, not as an error page, so an insert that fails mid-stream loses the verdict along with it. The visitor's case is decided before anything is written.

Logs travel further than the database: to files, to the host's log service, to whoever reads them to find a fault. What a log needs, to show the desk working, is what happened, not what the student asked.

The pieces a page shows are a reply on its way, and only the verdict says whether it stands. A stream that stops without one leaves the page to keep what it has.
