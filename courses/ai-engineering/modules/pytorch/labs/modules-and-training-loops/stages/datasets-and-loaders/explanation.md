With `shuffle=True`, each pass over a `DataLoader` deals the digits in a new order: the {% $fig.training %} training digits come as {% $fig.full %} batches of {% $fig.batch %} and a last of {% $fig.last %}. Each order is a permutation drawn from the loader's generator, so it depends on the seed and on how many epochs came before it, and on nothing else. Without a generator of its own, the loader draws from PyTorch's global one, which a layer's start, dropout and anything else random draw from too: change any of them, and every order after it changes.

`evaluate` switches the reader to evaluation before it reads. Batch normalization then normalizes by its running estimates, so each digit reads as it would in any batch, and nothing moves them. `torch.no_grad()` is a different switch: it stops autograd recording a graph, and leaves the mode alone. Under `torch.no_grad()` in training mode, batch normalization still mixes each batch's statistics into its running estimates, so reading for validation needs both switches.

The loss is a mean over digits, not over batches. Summed digit by digit and divided by their number, a short last batch counts as much per digit as the others. A mean of each batch's mean would count its digits for more.
