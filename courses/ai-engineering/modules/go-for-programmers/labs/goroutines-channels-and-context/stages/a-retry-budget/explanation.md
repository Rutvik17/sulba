Retries are load. Google's SRE book, on handling overload, works through what they do when every backend is overloaded: retrying each request up to three times means "the number of requests will grow significantly, to somewhere just below 3X", on servers that are failing because they have too much to do. Its answer is two limits. "First, we implement a per-request retry budget of up to three attempts", which is the backoff's `Attempts`. "Secondly, we implement a per-client retry budget. Each client keeps track of the ratio of requests that correspond to retries. A request will only be retried as long as this ratio is below 10%." With it, the book says, retries' growth falls "to just 1.1x in the general case".

A ratio alone would refuse the first retry of a client that has sent one request. Twitter's Finagle, whose clients retry out of a budget like this one, adds a floor: its default allows "approximately 20% of the total requests to be retried on top of 10 retries per second in order to accommodate clients that have just started issuing requests or clients that have a low rate of requests per second." The gateway's budget is the same shape, a floor plus a ratio of the requests, counted over a window.

The window is what lets the budget recover. An outage spends it, and the requests and retries the outage made leave the window a minute later, so a quiet client gets its retries back. A budget that counted every request it had ever seen would remember an outage for as long as the gateway ran.

`RetryWithin` asks the budget only for a retry it would really make: after it has checked the request's own attempts and the caller's deadline, and before it waits, so a refusal costs the caller no time and spends no budget.
