`rowsBetween(-6, 0)` would take the zone's last seven rows, and "A row based boundary is based on the position of the row within the partition", whatever days those rows fall on. `rangeBetween(-6, 0)` takes the rows whose ordering value is within 6 of the current one: "A range-based boundary is based on the actual value of the ORDER BY expression(s)", and that expression "must have a numerical data type", which is why the window orders by the number of days since 1 August rather than the date itself.

The difference shows wherever a zone has days without pickups. {% $fig.example %} had clean pickups on {% $fig.example_days %} days in August. Over the seven days to 15 August it had {% $fig.example_week %} trips, a mean of {% $fig.example_mean %} a day. Its last seven rows on 15 August reach back to {% $fig.example_rows_from %}, and average {% $fig.example_rows_mean %}.

Summing over the frame and dividing by 7 counts a day with no row as 0. `avg` over the same frame would divide by the rows it found, as if the zone had been busy on its quiet days.

`rank()` gives tied zones one place and skips the places after them: its documentation says "dense_rank leaves no gaps in ranking sequence when there are ties", and `rank` leaves them. The zone lookup is a few hundred rows against millions of trips, and `broadcast` "Marks a DataFrame as small enough for use in broadcast joins", in which, as Spark's tuning guide puts it, a table is "broadcast to all worker nodes when performing a join". It is a hint: "there is no guarantee that Spark will choose the join strategy specified in the hint".
